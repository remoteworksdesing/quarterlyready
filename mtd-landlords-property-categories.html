MTD for landlords: the UK property categories explained

HMRC gives landlords 4 income and 9 expense categories for quarterly updates. Here is what goes in each, and the four rules that trip people up.

Updated 12 September 2026 · Category names are taken verbatim from HMRC's MTD Update Notice (27 March 2026).

Who this applies to

If your gross rental income (before expenses, your share only) plus any self-employment turnover was over £50,000 on your 2024/25 return, you are in MTD for Income Tax from April 2026. Over £30,000 joins in April 2027 and over £20,000 in April 2028. All your UK property is treated as one "property business" and gets one quarterly update, however many properties you own.

Source: gov.uk – Check if you're eligible.

Income categories

HMRC categoryWhat goes here
Total rentRent received from tenants, including from lodgers unless you use Rent-a-Room relief. Your share only for joint property.
Other income from propertyService charges you bill tenants, deposit deductions you keep, insurance payouts for lost rent, ground rent you receive as a freeholder.
Premiums for the grant of a leaseLump sums received for granting a lease of 50 years or less. Rare for most landlords.
Reverse premiums and inducementsPayments you receive from a landlord or developer for taking on a lease. Also rare.

Expense categories

HMRC categoryWhat goes here
Rent, rates, insurance and ground rentsLandlord insurance, ground rent and service charges you pay as a leaseholder, council tax and utilities during void periods.
Property repairs and maintenanceRepairs, redecoration, boiler servicing, replacing like-for-like (a broken window, a dead boiler). Not improvements – an extension or a first-ever central heating system is capital, not a repair.
Non-residential property finance costsMortgage interest and arrangement fees on commercial lets. Fully deductible.
Residential property finance costsMortgage interest and arrangement fees on residential lets. Reported here, not deducted from profit – see below.
Residential finance costs brought forwardUnused residential finance costs carried forward from earlier years (they arise when your relief was capped by low profits).
Legal, management and other professional feesLetting agent fees, tenancy agreement drafting, inventory clerks, accountant's fees for the rental business, eviction costs. Not the legal costs of buying or selling the property.
Costs of services provided, including wagesCleaners, gardeners, caretakers, utilities you supply to tenants and cannot recharge.
Travel expensesJourneys to inspect, maintain or let the property. Flat-rate mileage (55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in 2026/27, 25p after) or actual costs – not both for the same vehicle.
Other allowable property expensesAdvertising for tenants, safety certificates, phone and stationery, replacement domestic items relief (the like-for-like replacement of furniture and appliances in a furnished let).

Source: HMRC Update Notice. Mileage rates: simplified expenses – vehicles.

The four rules that trip people up

1. Residential mortgage interest is not an expense

Since 2020, finance costs on residential lets are not deducted from rental profit. You report them in their own category and receive a tax reduction of 20% of the finance costs (capped at your property profit and your total income). Two consequences: your "profit" for the update looks higher than your cash position, and a basic-rate taxpayer is broadly unaffected while a higher-rate taxpayer pays more than under the old rules. A tracker that silently deducts interest as an expense will overstate your relief.

2. Joint property: your share only

If you own with a spouse or partner you each report your own share – 50/50 by default for married couples unless you have made a declaration otherwise. HMRC also gives jointly-let landlords an easement: report income only in the quarterly updates and add expenses in a re-sent Q4 update if that is easier. The final return still needs everything.

3. Furnished holiday lets are gone

The FHL regime was abolished from 6 April 2025. If you had a holiday let it is now part of your ordinary UK property business: same categories, same update, and the old FHL perks (capital allowances on furniture, pension-relevant earnings) no longer apply.

Source: gov.uk – Abolition of the FHL regime.

4. The £1,000 property allowance is either/or

If your gross property income is under £1,000 it is tax-free and you need not report it at all. Above that, you may deduct a flat £1,000 instead of your actual expenses – useful for a low-cost let – but never both, and not alongside Rent-a-Room relief. If you claim the allowance, HMRC does not need your expense categories for the property source; you report income only.

Source: gov.uk – Tax-free allowances on property and trading income.

Cash basis is now the default

Landlords use the cash basis by default: count rent when it arrives and expenses when you pay them. That makes bank-statement bookkeeping straightforward and matches how most people think about their lets. You can elect traditional (accruals) accounting instead; if you do, tell your software.

FAQ

Where does mortgage interest go?

Residential lets: "Residential property finance costs" – reported, not deducted, 20% tax reduction instead. Commercial lets: "Non-residential property finance costs" – fully deductible.

Is a new kitchen a repair?

Replacing a worn-out kitchen with a broadly similar one is a repair. Upgrading to something materially better, or adding what was not there before, is capital and does not go in the quarterly update.

Can I claim my own time managing the property?

No. Only money actually spent is an expense. Your own labour is not deductible.

Do I report the deposit I hold?

No. A protected deposit is the tenant's money. Only amounts you keep at the end of a tenancy (for damage or arrears) become income, under "Other income from property".
