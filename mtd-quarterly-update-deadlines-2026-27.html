MTD quarterly update deadlines 2026/27

The four dates, what each update has to contain, the calendar-quarter option, and what actually happens if you miss one.

Updated 12 September 2026 · Sources: HMRC guidance on gov.uk, linked in each section.

The four deadlines

If you are mandated into Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2026, you send HMRC a quarterly update for each self-employed business and one for your whole UK property business. Each update is due one month and seven days after the end of the period it covers.

Notice that each period starts on the same day. That is because updates are cumulative: Q2 is not "July to September", it is "the tax year so far". More on why that matters below.

What an update actually contains

An update is a set of totals, not a list of transactions. For each business you send the year-to-date total of each income and expense category HMRC recognises – for self-employment that is 2 income and 13 expense categories; for UK property 4 and 9. You do not send receipts, invoices or bank statements, and you do not pay anything.

If your turnover is below the VAT threshold (£90,000) HMRC will accept a single "income" total and a single "expenses" total instead of the full categories. Above it, full categorisation is compulsory. Full categories are accepted either way and save you re-sorting everything at year-end.

Why "cumulative" is good news

Because every update restates the year to date, a mistake in an earlier quarter does not need a resubmission. Find a missed expense from May in November? Add it to your records; the Q3 update in February carries the corrected total automatically. The only figures HMRC ultimately relies on are the ones in your final return.

Standard or calendar quarters?

By default the periods run from the 6th to the 5th, matching the tax year. If your bank statements, VAT returns or bookkeeping already run to calendar month-ends, you can elect calendar update periods instead: 1 April – 30 June, and so on to 31 March. The deadlines are identical.

Two things to know. The election is made in your MTD software before your first update of the year and cannot be changed once an update has been sent. And with calendar periods the few days from 1 to 5 April sit in the following year's first update rather than the current year's last one – your software handles this, but it is why a tracker needs to know which basis you chose.

What happens if you are late

MTD uses a points system: one point for each late submission, and a £200 penalty when you reach four points. Points expire after 24 months of good behaviour. For 2026/27 specifically, HMRC has confirmed it will not issue points for late quarterly updates – a soft-landing year while people get used to the routine. Late final returns and late payment are still penalised: 3% of the tax at 15 days late, another 3% at 30 days, then 10% a year on top.

So a missed quarterly update in 2026/27 will not cost you money. It will cost you a scramble later, because the year-end return still has to reconcile.

Source: gov.uk – Penalties for MTD for Income Tax.

After Q4: the final return

The Q4 update on 7 May 2027 is not the end. You still file a final tax return for 2026/27 – through your MTD software rather than the old Self Assessment form – and pay what you owe by 31 January 2028. That return is where you add anything the quarterly updates do not cover: other income, reliefs, capital allowances, adjustments.

A simple quarter-end routine

  1. Reconcile your bank statement to the period end (5th or month-end).
  2. Check every transaction has a date, an amount and an HMRC category.
  3. Read off the cumulative totals per category.
  4. Move them into your MTD-compatible or bridging software by digital link – cell links, file import or CSV, never retyped.
  5. Submit, and note the date you did it.

Done well this takes twenty minutes a quarter. Done badly – shoebox of receipts, categories guessed on the night of the 6th – it takes an evening and produces numbers you cannot defend.

FAQ

What is the deadline for the Q2 update in 2026?

7 November 2026, covering 6 April to 5 October (or 1 April to 30 September on calendar quarters).

Do I send one update or several?

One per self-employed business, plus one covering all your UK property together. Two trades and a rental portfolio means three updates each quarter.

I'm jointly letting a property with my spouse.

Each of you reports your own share. HMRC also allows jointly-let landlords to report income only in the quarterly updates and add expenses in a re-sent Q4 update.

Is there a penalty for a late quarterly update in 2026/27?

No points will be issued for late quarterly updates in 2026/27. The final return and payment deadlines are still enforced.

Do I pay tax quarterly now?

No. Quarterly updates are information only. Payment dates have not changed.
