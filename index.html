Quarterly update deadlines for 2026/27

Every update is cumulative – it covers the tax year to date – and is due one month and seven days after the period ends. You send one update per business plus one for your whole property business. No tax is paid at these dates.

Can I keep using a spreadsheet?

Yes – and this is the part most people get wrong. HMRC's own guidance says you can continue to use spreadsheets for your records, provided you use bridging software (or other MTD-compatible software) that links to the spreadsheet to make the submission. Three rules:

  1. Each transaction needs a date, an amount and a category – HMRC's category, not your own.
  2. Totals must reach your submission software by a digital link: linked cells, file import or CSV. Retyping figures by hand is not allowed.
  3. Below the £90,000 VAT threshold you may record just "income" and "expenses"; above it, full categories are compulsory. Full categories are accepted either way and make the year-end return easier.

Sources: gov.uk – Using MTD for Income Tax · Create digital records · Update Notice (category list).

FAQ

Are quarterly updates cumulative?

Yes. Each update covers the tax year to date. If you made a mistake in Q1, correct your records and the Q2 update carries the right figures – no resubmission needed.

What if I have two businesses, or a business and rental property?

One quarterly update per self-employed business, plus one for your entire UK property business. The tracker keeps self-employment and property as separate sources with separate summaries.

Do I have to pay tax quarterly?

No. Quarterly updates are information only. Tax is still paid by 31 January (with payments on account where they apply).

I'm jointly letting a property – what do I report?

Your share only. HMRC also lets jointly-let landlords send income only in the quarterly updates and add expenses in a re-sent Q4 update.

What about furnished holiday lets?

The FHL regime ended on 6 April 2025. Former FHLs are now part of your ordinary UK property business and use the same categories.

Is the £1,000 trading / property allowance still available?

Yes. You may deduct £1,000 instead of actual expenses for a source (never both). If you claim it, HMRC does not need your expense figures for that source.
