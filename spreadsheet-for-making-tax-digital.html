Can I use a spreadsheet for Making Tax Digital?

Yes. Here are the three conditions HMRC attaches, what "digital link" and "bridging software" mean, and the mistakes that get people into trouble.

Updated 12 September 2026 · Sources: HMRC guidance on gov.uk, linked in each section.

The short answer

HMRC's own words: if you use spreadsheets for your records, you can continue to use them – but you'll still need software that links to your spreadsheets, sometimes called bridging software. Making Tax Digital is not "buy accounting software or else". It is "keep digital records and submit through software". A spreadsheet is a digital record. What it cannot do is talk to HMRC on its own.

Source: gov.uk – Using Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

Condition 1: the records themselves

Every transaction must be recorded digitally with three things: the date, the amount, and the category of income or expense. A bank export with the category added counts. A photo of a receipt does not, on its own – it needs a row somewhere with those three fields.

The categories are HMRC's, published in the MTD Update Notice: 2 income and 13 expense categories for self-employment, 4 and 9 for UK property. If your turnover is under the VAT threshold of £90,000 you may collapse these to a single "income" and a single "expense" figure. Above it, full categorisation is compulsory. There is no downside to using the full categories from the start; the year-end return needs them anyway.

Sources: Create digital records · Update Notice · Digital record keeping notice.

Condition 2: the digital link

This is the rule that catches people. When your totals move from the spreadsheet to the software that submits them, the transfer has to be digital: linked cells between workbooks, importing the file, exporting and importing a CSV, or emailing the file to whoever submits for you. HMRC gives one explicit "must not": you must not manually move the record. Reading a total off your screen and typing it into a submission form breaks the chain.

In practice this means your spreadsheet needs a clean summary – one row per HMRC category, one column per quarter – that a bridging tool can point at. Scattered subtotals across five tabs will make you retype, and retyping is the thing that is not allowed.

Source: gov.uk – Create digital records (digital links section).

Condition 3: bridging software

Five mistakes to avoid

  1. Your own category names. "Van stuff" is not an HMRC category. Use their wording so the totals map straight across.
  2. Mixing sources in one list. Self-employment and property are separate updates. Keep them on separate tabs with separate summaries.
  3. Non-cumulative quarters. Q2 is April to September, not July to September. If your sheet only sums the last three months you will under-report.
  4. Deducting residential mortgage interest as an expense. Landlords report it in its own category; it is not deducted from profit but gives a 20% tax reduction instead.
  5. Retyping. The summary must be linked or imported into the bridging tool. If you find yourself typing totals into a form, stop and set up the link.

What a good MTD spreadsheet looks like

  • A transaction log per source with a date, an HMRC category dropdown, an amount and a description.
  • Automatic quarter assignment from the date – on your chosen basis, standard or calendar.
  • A summary tab with cumulative totals per category per quarter, in one clean block a bridging tool can read.
  • The deadlines visible, so you are not calculating "one month and seven days" in your head.
  • Rates and thresholds in editable cells with the HMRC source noted, so you can update them next April instead of buying a new file.

FAQ

Is Excel "MTD-compatible software"?

No. Excel and Google Sheets are record-keeping tools. They become part of an MTD-compliant setup when paired with bridging software that links to them.

Can my accountant submit from my spreadsheet?

Yes. Emailing them the file is an accepted digital link; they then submit through their own software.

Do I need to keep the spreadsheet for years?

Yes – the usual record-keeping rules still apply (five years after the 31 January deadline for self-employed and landlords). Keep a copy of each year's file.

What if I'm under the £90,000 threshold – can I just do income and expenses?

You can. But the final return still asks for the category breakdown, so most people find it easier to categorise as they go.
